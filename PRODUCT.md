# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Busy, non-technical business partners — founders, program directors, account leads at
SEPARATE partner companies (e.g. a SaaS company + a nonprofit + a marketing agency running
a joint go-to-market). They meet weekly, carry real workloads elsewhere, and will simply
stop opening any tool that costs them effort. Context: a 90-second glance between meetings,
often on a phone; the weekly ritual of pasting meeting notes; checking "what does my company
owe the partnership this week?"

## Product Purpose

Crossline turns a partnership's weekly meeting notes into action items owned by the RIGHT
company, tracked to done across all partners as equals (no host/guest hierarchy). One shared
page per partnership: paste the notes, approve the extracted commitments, watch the board.
Success = all three partner orgs actually open it every week without being chased — adoption
IS the metric. The visible magic: messy transcript in, clean owned commitments out.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, effortless. "The anti-Notion": where generalist tools sell flexibility
(and deliver chrome), Crossline sells one page that already knows what you need. Warm and
human rather than corporate; confident rather than feature-proud. Plain language everywhere:
tasks, done, this week — never jargon, never config. The name evokes a direct line that
crosses company lines.

## Anti-references

- **Notion / ClickUp / Monday / Asana**: the flexibility-as-chrome aesthetic. Sidebars of
  features, databases, view switchers, settings gears. Crossline's founding complaint.
- **Generic AI-tool slop**: purple gradients, glassmorphism, hero metrics, identical card
  grids, Inter-on-white sameness.
- **Enterprise dashboard**: dense KPI walls, filter bars, "workspace admin" energy.
- Persistent companion panels must reduce the viewport, not overlay it (owner preference).

## Design Principles

- **One page, nothing more.** Every element must justify itself to a distracted non-technical
  user in seconds. If it needs explaining, it's cut. No settings surface in v1.
- **Ownership is the interface.** WHO owes WHAT is the product; the partner companies (their
  names, their identity) structure the page — not abstract lists or statuses.
- **The magic moment is sacred.** Paste → owned commitments is the adoption hook; it gets the
  strongest design investment (state, motion, reveal) of any flow.
- **Equals at the table.** Peer companies, visually equal weight. No org is the "host". No
  org is an afterthought.
- **Warm precision.** Calm, human, quietly premium. Delight through restraint and craft,
  not decoration.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Non-technical users are the accessibility case: large tap targets (mobile-first checking),
plain-language labels, no icon-only affordances for primary actions, obvious focus states.
Aim for WCAG AA contrast. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Dark mode supported from the start
(token-level), light-first.
